\begin{abstract}
The $n$-string braid space is defined as a universal covering of the complex $n$-space branching at the hyperplanes $z_i=z_j$.
It is conjectured that $n$-string braid space is CAT(0) for any $n$.
We describe a source of convex functions in the braid space and use them to prove several special cases of this conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $\CC^n$ be the complex space
with coordinates $z_1,\dots,z_n$ and the standard Euclidean metric.
Remove from $\CC^n$ the complex hyperplanes $z_i=z_j$ for all $i\ne j$;
denote the remaining set by $W$.
Let $\tilde W$ be the universal cover of $W$, equipped with the induced metric.
The completion of $\tilde W$ will be called the \emph{braid space}
with $n$ strings and denoted by $\CB^n$.
(Note that $W$ can be interpreted as configuration space of $n$-tuples of distinct points in the plane.
Therefore a path in $W$ describes a braid with $n$ strings, which explains our terminology.)

The following conjecture is a special case of a conjecture formulated by Daniel Allcock~\cite[Conjecture 1.4]{allcock}, which is related to an earlier conjecture of Ruth Charney and Michael Davis (see \cite[Conjecture 3]{charney-davis-95}), which in turn is motivated by the $K(\pi,1)$-conjecture \cite{vanderlek,paris}.
The authors are responsible for a more general version of Allcock's conjecture \cite[Conjecture 1.1]{panov-petrunin}, which we have been trying to prove for several years.

\begin{thm}{Conjecture}\label{braid-conj}
$\CB^n$ is $\CAT(0)$ for any integer $n\ge 1$.
\end{thm}

The case $n=2$ is easy.
The case $n=3$ is already nontrivial;
two proofs are given by the authors \cite{panov-petrunin};
one of them follows from the result of Ruth Charney and Michael Davis \cite{charney-davis-93}.
Also Ruth Charney proved a special case of the conjecture for $n=4$ \cite[Theorem~5.4]{charney2004}.
Namely, she proved that real braid space with 4 strings is $\CAT(0)$.
One may think of the real braid space as the space of braid diagrams.
Formally, it is the inverse image of $\RR^n$ under the natural projection $\CB^n\to \CC^n$.
This is a convex subset of $\CB^n$; so if the braid space is $\CAT(0)$, then so is the corresponding real braid space.

In this note we construct a class of convex functions on the braid space, which will be called \emph{pull-tight}.
The idea is inspired by Ian Agol's answer to our question \cite{agol}.

A point in $W$ is the complex plane $\CC$ with $n$ punctures.
Choose two ideal points in the complex plane $\CC$ and a simple curve that connects them in the punctured plane.
Think that the curve is a thread; let us pull its ends off so it becomes tight.
The curve becomes a polygonal line with vertices at the punctures and two infinite edges and possible self-intersections.
It can be arbitrarily well approximated by a simple curve that does not pass thru the punctures.
Altho the obtained curve has infinite length, one can find the excess of its length by comparing it to the two-edge curve passing thru the origin --- the construction is similar to the construction of а Busemann function.
This excess defines a locally Lipschitz function $W\to\RR$, which can be extended to a function $\CB^n\to\RR$.
The functions that can be obtained this way will be called \emph{pull-tight}.

\begin{thm}{Pull-tight theorem}\label{thm:pull-tight}
Every pull-tight function $\CB^n\to\RR$ is convex.
\end{thm}

The braid space $\CB^n$ has a natural structure of a polyhedral space.
Therefore, to show that $\CB^n$ is $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ one has to forbid geodesic digons \cite[3.4.3]{AKP-invitation}.
The pull-tight theorem implies the following weaker statement.

\begin{thm}{Theorem}\label{thm:no-loops}
The braid space has no geodesic loops.
\end{thm}

Assuming a geodesic loop exists, we find a pull-tight function that is not constant on the loop and arrive at a contradiction.


\section{Pull-tight functions}

\parit{Proof of \ref{thm:pull-tight}.}
Let $f\:\CB^n\to \RR$ be a pull-tight function.
Choose a unit-speed geodesic $\gamma$ in $\CB^n$;
we need to show that $f\circ\gamma$ is a convex real-to-real function.

Each point $\gamma(t)$ defines an array of points $p_1(t),\ldots,p_m(t)\in\CC$.
The points move with constant velocity between the collision times.

Denote by $\ell(t)$ the pull-tight curve that corresponds to $f$.
It is a polygonal line that can be encoded by a sequence of its vertices; each vertex must be one of the points $p_i(t)$;  self-intersections, repeated vertices and edges are allowed.

Suppose that the vertex sequence of $\ell(t)$ does not change in some open interval $\II\subset\RR$.
Then convexity of $f\circ\gamma|_{\II}$ follows from the convexity of the distance function between two geodesics in the plane.

The vertex sequence can change in two ways:  (1) when one of the points hits or leaves $\ell(t)$ and (2) at the collision of points.
We may assume that such events happened only finitely many times.
If it happens at time $t_0$ then we have to check that
\[(f\circ\gamma)^+(t_0) - (f\circ\gamma)^-(t_0)\ge 0;\leqno({*})\]
once it is done convexity of $f\circ\gamma$ follows.

In the first case there is no collision, but vertex sequence changes.
(For example, at time $t_0$ we may add a new point $p_i(t)$ between vertices $p_j(t)$ and $p_k(t)$.)
In a neighborhood of $t_0$ the curve is described by two vertex sequences;
denote the corresponding functions by $f_{\textrm{before}}$ and $f_{\textrm{after}}$;
if $t\approx t_0$, then
\[f\circ\gamma(t)=
\begin{cases}
f_{\textrm{before}}\circ\gamma(t)&\text{if\ } t\le t_0,
\\
f_{\textrm{after}}\circ\gamma(t)&\text{if\ } t\ge t_0.
\end{cases}
\]
Note that
\[(f_{\textrm{before}}\circ\gamma)'(t_0)=(f_{\textrm{after}}\circ\gamma)'(t_0)\]
hence $({*})$ follows.

In the second case we can assume that $k$ points collide at time $t_0$.
We can assume that $t_0=0$ and the collision point is also $0\in\CC$.

Rescale a small neighborhood of the collision point and a small time interval around $0$, keeping the velocities the same.
In the limit we get a collision of all $k$ points in $\CB^k$.
The points come with constant velocities to $0$ and run from it with constant velocities.
We need to check $({*})$ for the corresponding function $\tilde f\colon\CB^k\to \RR$.
Denote by $\tilde \ell(t)$ and $\tilde \gamma$ the corresponding pull-tight curve and geodesic in $\CB^k$.

Note that the shape of $\tilde \ell(t)$ does not change: for $t>0$ and $t<0$ it only rescales and at $t=0$ it approaches an angle from both sides.
If $({*})$ does not hold then adding a constant vector to all velocities in the $k$-point array can reduce the problem to the case \[(\tilde f\circ\tilde \gamma)^-(0)>0>(\tilde f\circ\tilde \gamma)^+(0).\]
In other words, the function $\tilde f\circ\tilde \gamma$ has a strict maximum at $0$.
This is impossible if the ideal points of $\tilde \ell$ are opposite;
further we assume that they are not and construct a shortcut of $\tilde \gamma$, showing that it was not geodesic.

Altho $\tilde\ell(t)$ might have self-intersections,
in the obvious generalized sense it bounds a region $D_t$, equipped with its intrinsic length metric.
Note that $D_t$ is $\CAT(0)$.


Choose an ideal point $\xi$ that lies midway between the ideal points of $\tilde\ell(t)$.
Note that superlevel sets of the Busemann function $b_t\:D_t\to\RR$ are compact.
After normalizing $b_0(0)=0$, the strict maximum above implies
\[\max\set{b_t(x)}{x\in D_t}\le -\eps\cdot|t|\]
for some $\eps>0$.

Now apply the gradient flow of $b_t$
(which is simply moving $x$ along the geodesic half-line from $x$ to $\xi$),
stopping at the sublevel set $\{b_t\le-\delta\}$ for some fixed $\delta>0$.
Since $D_t$ is $\CAT(0)$, this map is length nonincreasing.
For sufficiently small $\delta$, this deformation is the identity at the
ends of a small interval around $0$ and provides a shortcut, proving that $\tilde\gamma$ is not a
geodesic --- a contradiction.
\qeds

\section{No geodesic loops}

\parit{Proof of \ref{thm:no-loops}.}
Let $\gamma\:[0,L]\to\CB^n$ be a geodesic loop;
denote by $p_1(t),\ldots,p_n(t)\z\in\CC$ its points at the time $t$.

Choose $n-1$ pull-tight functions $f_1,\ldots,f_{n-1}$
with fixed two opposite ideal points
such that the corresponding pull-tight curves are straight lines that cut off $1,\ldots,n-1$ points, respectively, from $p_1(0),\ldots,p_n(0)$.

With natural the normalization we have
\[f_i\circ\gamma(0)=f_i\circ\gamma(L)=0\]
for each $i$.
Since $f_i$ are convex, $f_i\circ\gamma(t)\equiv 0$ for all $i$.
The latter implies that the image of $\gamma$ in $\CC$ is a line segment --- a contradiction.
\qeds
